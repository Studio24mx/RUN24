# RUN24 — Art Direction Proposals

Status: exploration before final visual lock.

## Decision criteria
Each direction is judged on: instant readability, international appeal, originality, character/fan-art potential, boss potential, marketing thumbnail strength, narrative depth, and feasibility for a zero-budget 2D production.

## Direction A — SIGNALPUNK: The Network of the Dead
**Projection: 9.3/10 — recommended**

### One-line hook
A run-and-gun roguelite where humanity connected technology to the afterlife and someone hijacked the signal of the dead.

### World
A retrofuturist Mexican megacity was built around THE SIGNAL, an infrastructure that routes memories and souls through an ancient underworld that existed long before the machines. When the network collapses, the dead stop moving on, memories leak into the living world, and corporate systems begin interpreting gods as software.

### Protagonist
RUNNER 24: the 24th living subject sent through the dead-network. Compact silhouette, masked face, dark body, luminous CORE in the chest. The number 24 is a diagnostic mark and a mystery.

### Companion
XOLO: a spectral-tech xoloitzcuintle. Black/obsidian body, bone-white mask elements, luminous glyphs, parts of the silhouette glitching between physical and spirit states. Guide, mascot and emotional anchor.

### Common enemies
- Signal husks: dead memories reconstructed incorrectly.
- Corporate psychopomps: machines built to route souls.
- Jaguar hunters: elite retrieval units.
- Broadcast saints: corrupted public-service idols that emit attacks as transmissions.

### Boss identity
Every boss is a person/entity with poster-level silhouette and a clear relationship to death, memory or ownership.
- THE IDOL: artificial celebrity transmitting to trapped souls.
- THE JAGUAR: corporate hunter able to cross signal layers.
- THE TWINS: two minds running one body.
- THE MOTHERBOARD: machine preserving millions of dead minds against their will.
- THE BLACK SUN: entity behind the network.

### Palette
- Obsidian: #0B0D14
- Bone: #F2E7D3
- Cochineal: #D92E5B
- Jade/Cyan: #35D5C8
- Cempasuchil: #F3A712
- Deep violet: #31204F

### Shape language
Hard geometric silhouettes plus organic spirit trails. Rectangles/circuit grids belong to technology; stepped, radial and glyph-like forms belong to the old underworld. Avoid copying archaeological motifs literally: reinterpret proportions and rhythm rather than tracing artifacts.

### UI
Diegetic diagnostic overlays mixed with codex-like panels. CORE uses jewel/glyph segments. Room changes feel like signal locks. Typography is condensed industrial sans for system text + a custom display face derived from stepped geometric forms.

### Animation
Primary characters: cutout/skeletal animation in Godot for locomotion, aim and reusable states.
Hero moments: limited hand-drawn accents in Krita (smears, face/cloth accents, boss transformations).
FX: particles, shader distortion, screen-space glitch, ink-like spirit trails.

### Gameplay-screen target
Dark architectural silhouettes with 2–3 strong value planes. Player reads immediately as cyan/bone against near-black. Enemies use cochineal/red. Interactable/good energy uses cempasuchil. Background lore can be dense, foreground combat must remain clean.

### Marketing strength
A screenshot should be identifiable by the obsidian/bone/jade/cochineal palette and XOLO even without the logo. Strongest balance between originality, story, combat readability and producibility.

### Main risk
Falling back into generic cyberpunk. Rule: no neon for decoration. Every luminous element must indicate soul-state, system-state, faction or gameplay function.

## Direction B — DEAD AIR: Latin Analog Folklore Horror
**Projection: 8.8/10**

### One-line hook
A rogue broadcast is turning urban legends, saints and forgotten voices into physical monsters.

### World
An alternate late-1980s/early-1990s Latin American city where emergency broadcast infrastructure began receiving transmissions from places that should not exist. Television towers, cassette archives, call centers, roadside shrines and government signal bunkers form the levels.

### Protagonist
RUNNER 24 is a field technician/containment operative wearing an oversized signal helmet and analog measuring gear. Visor becomes the iconic face.

### Companion
A tiny CRT-headed creature nicknamed PULGA that receives voices before the player can hear them.

### Common enemies
- VHS ghosts with tracking-error anatomy.
- Telephone saints.
- Antenna-headed civilians.
- Emergency mascot creatures.
- Broadcast insects made from magnetic tape.

### Bosses
- THE ANCHOR: smiling news presenter whose face changes between frames.
- CHANNEL 0: a transmission represented by multiple bodies.
- THE CALLER: entity living inside phone lines.
- LA TORRE: living broadcast mast.

### Palette
- Charcoal: #171717
- Dirty ivory: #D8D0BC
- Emergency red: #D83A35
- CRT green: #67C587
- Sodium yellow: #D8A33E
- Tape blue: #405E78

### Shape language
Chunky analog electronics, 4:3 frames, scanlines, imperfect registration, photocopy texture, public-information iconography, handmade warning signs.

### UI
CRT diagnostic UI, timecode, tracking indicators, tape labels and stamped warnings.

### Animation
Extremely production-friendly: intentionally limited poses, stepped frame rates, frame holds and distortion effects become part of the aesthetic. Great fit for small team.

### Gameplay-screen target
More horror than power fantasy. Strong darkness and pools of sodium/CRT light. Enemies may partially exist only when signal interference reveals them.

### Marketing strength
Excellent streamer/YouTube hook and very distinctive screenshots. Latin urban folklore gives authenticity without depending on ancient-Mexico imagery.

### Main risk
The horror tone can conflict with fast heroic run-and-gun action. Needs humor and kinetic bosses to avoid becoming visually oppressive.

## Direction C — AFTERLIFE INC.: Cute Macabre Bureaucracy
**Projection: 8.6/10**

### One-line hook
Death has become a broken megacorporation, and RUNNER 24 is the employee sent to clear the backlog of souls by force.

### World
A colorful afterlife bureaucracy where every cosmic process is run by departments: Admissions, Memory Processing, Reincarnation, Lost Souls, Final Exit. The system is collapsing under impossible quotas.

### Protagonist
RUNNER 24 is a small masked courier/repo agent with a huge badge, tiny body and oversized weapon-core. Most immediately toyetic version.

### Companion
XOLO becomes an overconfident office guide wearing a tie/tag that never sits correctly.

### Common enemies
- Stamp goblins.
- Skeleton interns.
- File-cabinet mimics.
- Smiling HR reapers.
- Paperwork swarms.

### Bosses
- THE MANAGER: many arms, each holding a different form.
- HR SERAPH: angelic compliance machine.
- THE AUDITOR: enormous eye/stamp entity.
- CEO MICTLAN: charming executive who owns the exit.

### Palette
- Ink black: #16131B
- Paper cream: #F5E9D3
- Marigold: #F5A623
- Candy magenta: #EC4C7A
- Mint jade: #49D6B0
- Office blue: #4A79C9

### Shape language
Round mascot bodies versus oppressive rectangular office machinery. Paper, stamps, folders and signage give us cheap modular environmental assets.

### UI
Forms, stamps, employee badges, approval/rejection animations. Health and CORE look like punched employee credentials.

### Animation
Cutout-heavy, squash-and-stretch, facial swaps, strong anticipation. Easier to make charming than realistic animation because exaggeration is expected.

### Gameplay-screen target
High contrast, bright readable silhouettes, strong comedy. Most accessible for broad audience and merchandising.

### Marketing strength
Highest mascot/merch/fan-art potential. Easy to communicate in GIFs and short clips.

### Main risk
The cute-macabre space is crowded after games such as Cult of the Lamb and Have a Nice Death. We would need unusually strong writing and Mexican/Latin specificity to avoid looking derivative.

## Recommendation
Build the first serious visual vertical slice in **SIGNALPUNK**. Keep **DEAD AIR** as a reserve direction if Signalpunk begins to look too generic in execution. Borrow one lesson from AFTERLIFE INC.: XOLO and NPCs must still have warmth, humor and strong mascot silhouettes even if the world is dark.

## Non-negotiable art rules for the Signalpunk test
1. No generic neon city backgrounds.
2. No direct copies of pre-Hispanic artifacts or gods as game characters.
3. Culture is expressed through design logic, worldbuilding, materials, rhythm, language and mythology—not decorative stereotypes.
4. Combat readability outranks background detail.
5. Every major character must be recognizable in solid black silhouette.
6. Every boss must have one screenshot-ready pose.
7. Limit a gameplay scene to one dominant neutral + two active accent colors.
8. Reuse modular components aggressively; spend hand-drawn animation only on moments players will remember.
